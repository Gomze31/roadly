# Roadly 1.0 — GPS laboratory results

22 November 2026, branch `integration/phase5` (uncommitted additions on top of `813d5a0`).

## What this laboratory is

Every scenario below runs the **production** `RouteTracker` and `NavigationSession` on the JVM. Positions are **computed** along
route geometries; no recorded GPS trace exists in the repository, and none was fabricated. Routes are of two kinds:

- **Real route** — a Valhalla response saved as a fixture (`motorway`: Toulouse A620, exits 31b and 19, four roundabouts;
  `muret`: two roundabouts; `nation` and `etoile`: Paris, 6th exit). Geometry and manoeuvres are real, positions are computed.
- **Synthetic route** — geometry built in the test (`TestRoutes`): straight, left turn, out-and-back, divided road, overpass, parallel roads, 500 km line.

Nothing here is a road test. Statuses: PASS = verified by the stated method; NOT TESTED = no test environment.

## Findings of this pass

No new defect required a code change in the navigation engine. Three behaviours are recorded as they are:

1. **Missing accuracy.** When the phone reports no accuracy, Roadly assumes 50 m. Above 30 m, « maintenant », off-route and arrival
   are all withheld by design. A phone that never reports accuracy would therefore get distance announcements but no « maintenant »
   and no arrival. Google's fused provider always reports accuracy (7.9–11.6 m measured on the HONOR), so this is a theoretical
   degraded mode, not a defect seen on the target device.
2. **Reversed heading.** With the GPS heading reversed for two fixes inside Place de la Nation, the displayed position jumps 97 m back
   for those two seconds (the heading filter discards the true segment and picks another pass of the route), then recovers; no reroute,
   nothing wrong is said. Reversed for ten fixes, a reroute is requested — consistent with a vehicle really driving the wrong way.
   Left unchanged: telling a glitch from a real wrong-way needs road evidence.
3. **Speed spike.** Speed is smoothed (factor 0.3). At 30 km/h a 250 km/h spike 200 m before a turn does not trigger « maintenant »
   early. Closer than about 110 m it would; not observed on any real trace, since there is none.

Defects found and fixed earlier in the same laboratory are listed in `docs/GUIDAGE-INTELLIGENT.md` §9.6, §10.1, §11 and §12.

## 22 November 2026 (later pass) — phone clock, coarse fixes, urban canyon, busy public server (audit NAV-2 to NAV-5)

Nine new scenarios in `lab/GpsTimeAndAccuracyLabTest`, all on **synthetic GPS** (positions computed along the real `nation` and
`motorway` routes) replayed in the production `NavigationSession`. **None was driven**; no phone, emulator or car was used. The
laboratory gained three things for them: a clock with two time bases (phone time that can be wrong or jump, monotonic time),
fixes stamped with a monotonic time as on a phone, and a router that goes through the real `ValhallaRateLimiter`.

Run: `:core:navigation:testDebugUnitTest` → 444 tests, 0 failure, 4 skipped (JVM). Each scenario below except the "guard"
failed before the corresponding change (for the three clock-jump scenarios the failure was observed with the laboratory clock
collapsed to a single time base, which reproduces the previous computation).

What changed in the engine:

- **Time (NAV-2).** Fix age, GPS silence, reroute back-off and voice delays use the monotonic clock (`SystemClock.elapsedRealtime()`
  and `Location.elapsedRealtimeNanos`). Phone time is kept only for what is shown or stored (arrival time, trip start and end,
  remembered guidance, toll reminder, parked vehicle).
- **Coarse fixes (NAV-3).** A fix with an accuracy worse than 100 m is treated by guidance as no fix at all.
- **Degraded rule (NAV-4).** After 10 consecutive fixes between 30 m and 100 m of accuracy that are consistent with each other,
  off-route is decided on the gap minus the accuracy (145 m instead of 100 m at 45 m), and arrival on 3 consecutive fixes in the
  arrival zone at walking pace or stopped. This supersedes finding 1 above for off-route and arrival; « maintenant » is still
  withheld above 30 m.
- **Public server (NAV-5).** A reroute cancels the background request in flight and takes the next slot; the cancelled request is
  replayed afterwards. The rate (1 request per second, one in flight) is unchanged.

Not covered: the thresholds (100 m, 10 fixes, 3 fixes) are reasoned, not measured — no real urban-canyon or tunnel trace exists.
Real accuracy values in a canyon, how often a phone delivers network positions in a tunnel, and the real server's behaviour when
a call is cancelled are NOT TESTED.
